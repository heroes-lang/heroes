A small SQL database engine made the way SQLite is: pages in a file, B-trees, a log written before the database, transactions and a planner.

The program is the database itself: it keeps the data in a file of its own and no server stands in between. It reads its SQL with a parser of its own: tables and indexes created and dropped, rows inserted, changed and deleted, queries with joins (the rows of two tables put together where they share a value), groups, ordering and limits, and NULL treated as SQL wants it, as an unknown that equals nothing, not even itself. The data lives in a file cut into pages of one size; every table and every index is a B-tree, a structure that keeps its keys in order inside the pages so that finding a row costs reading a few of them. A transaction is a group of changes that holds whole or not at all. Every change is first appended to a separate log, the write-ahead log, with a checksum for each page: after a sudden stop the program reads the log again, keeps the transactions that finished and drops the one left halfway. One connection writes at a time, under a lock on a file beside the database, while the others read their own picture of the data without waiting. The planner, the part that decides how to read a table, chooses a search by key or by index over reading everything, and EXPLAIN shows its choice in SQLite’s words.

The output on this page is its demo: a shop’s tables, a few hundred orders made by a join, the trees they grow into, joins and groups, the cases where NULL surprises, the plans of EXPLAIN, constraints broken and refused, a transaction rolled back, a second connection kept out by the writer’s lock, then two simulated crashes, one after a commit and one in the middle of a transaction, each followed by the recovery of the log, an integrity check and the copy of the log into the database file. Rebel’s scanner reads the SQL, sql gives the values, the rows and the drivers’ contract, containers/cache keeps the pages used lately in memory, hash computes the checksums, fs and io read, write and lock the files, and csv and json write results in those formats.
