A realtime chat service and its terminal client: rooms, direct messages, history, emoji shortcodes, and bridges to MQTT and NATS.

People sign in with a password and get a signed token, then talk in rooms and in direct messages over a WebSocket, a connection that stays open so messages arrive as they are written. They see who is online and who is typing, page back through a room’s history kept in SQLite, and write :emoji_name: shortcodes that become emoji by their Unicode names. The terminal client edits a line by what a reader sees as one character, so a flag or a family is one step. A room can be mirrored both ways to MQTT or NATS, two kinds of message broker, the servers devices and services exchange messages through, so they can talk in it too.

The output on this page is its demo: a whole session played on the machine itself, the two brokers replaced by stand-ins the program carries. Rebel’s websocket, web and http carry the conversation, sql/sqlite keeps the history, jose signs the tokens, mqtt and nats are the bridge, tui draws the client’s screen, and unicode/segment and unicode/names handle the text.
