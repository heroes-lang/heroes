A background job system on Redis, as Sidekiq and Celery are: jobs enqueued, claimed by worker threads, retried, scheduled and traced.

Producers enqueue typed jobs into Redis, a fast database kept in memory and often used as a queue; a supervisor and a fleet of worker threads claim them and run each kind’s function, retry the failures with growing waits and a little randomness, keep the jobs that fail for good as dead letters to look at later, enqueue recurring jobs on cron schedules, the time patterns Unix uses for repeated tasks, and give back the jobs of workers that died. Every attempt is counted and timed in metrics served for Prometheus, each job is traced from its enqueue to its last attempt over OTLP, and each thread writes log files that rotate.

The output on this page is its demo: stand-ins the program carries, started on the machine itself, of a Redis server that runs its scripts, an OpenTelemetry collector and the web addresses the jobs call, on a clock moved by hand, printing a fixed account. Rebel’s jobs and jobs/redis_store keep the jobs and their states, redis speaks to the server, retries spaces the attempts, threads runs the fleet, containers/heap orders the timers, and metrics, tracing and otlp report.
