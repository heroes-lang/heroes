Stock management for a small chain of shops, one model of the books kept in MySQL, in a database reached through ODBC, or in MongoDB.

It keeps the chain’s locations, its products and their variants, every variant’s stock at every location, and every movement of stock, from receipts and sales to transfers, adjustments and counts, with an audit trail of everything asked of it, refusals included. It values stock at moving average cost in exact cents, tells which shelves are at or below their reorder point, imports catalogues and count sheets from CSV and XLSX, telling every rejected row with its line and reason, and exports the stock the same two ways. The operator chooses the store in the configuration: MySQL or MariaDB, any database the system’s ODBC manager reaches, ODBC being the standard door through which a program reaches many kinds of database, or MongoDB.

The output on this page is its demo: a MySQL server and a MongoDB server that the program carries, started on the machine itself, one scripted day run against each store, the account printed once with a digest of what each store gave, the same for every store, then files full of bad rows imported and the stock exported. The ODBC store runs where the machine has SQLite’s ODBC driver; elsewhere its one line says it was skipped, the one line that may differ between machines. Rebel’s sql/mysql, sql/odbc and mongodb reach the stores, sql/build composes the queries, csv and xlsx read and write the files, and uuid makes every key.
