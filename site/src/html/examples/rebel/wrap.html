Text folded to a width, written again with Rebel’s unicode/segment, keeping the program’s own rules for where a line breaks.

Rebel’s unicode/segment folds the text, as many words on a line as the width allows, and measures the widest line; its strings cuts the text into words. The program keeps its four rules and asks them of Rebel: a word that fits exactly stays on the line, a word longer than the width is left whole on a line of its own because breaking a web address is worse than a long line, any run of spaces and line breaks counts as one space, and no line ends in a space.

On the original’s sample the two print the same bytes. They part elsewhere: Rebel breaks wherever Unicode’s rules for breaking lines allow, after a hyphen too, and counts a line’s width in the columns a terminal draws, where the original breaks at spaces alone and counts bytes.
