A small POSIX shell with pipelines, redirections, functions, background jobs and a line editor, one program that also answers as small tools, as BusyBox does.

It reads the language the Unix shells share, POSIX being the standard they follow: quoting, variables and their default forms, command substitution, arithmetic and globs. It runs pipelines of any length, each program a child process joined to the next by a pipe, with redirections, here-documents, && and ||, if, while, for and case, functions, builtins such as cd, export and jobs, and background jobs started with &. On a terminal it edits each line with a history and completes with Tab, and a mistake is told with the line and the column of the script it is in. The same binary acts as small tools of its own when run under their names, as BusyBox does.

The output on this page is its demo: the session shown under Input, commands read from the standard input, played in a folder of its own. Rebel’s process starts and waits for every child, io carries the pipes and the redirections, term is the line editor, regex turns the shell’s patterns into regular expressions that match in linear time, and unicode/segment places the caret under a mistake.
