An IoT hub: devices on MQTT, AMQP, NATS or a serial line, their readings decoded, re-published as JSON and routed between protocols.

Devices publish to an MQTT broker, an AMQP broker and a NATS server, three kinds of server devices send their messages through, or speak NMEA 0183 on a serial line. The hub reads them all, finds each device in its registry by the address it publishes at, decodes its payload by the registry’s encoding and schema, SenML in CBOR, MessagePack maps, or Protocol Buffers read from .proto files while it runs, re-publishes every reading as JSON, and routes readings from one protocol to another by rules with conditions. A dashboard watches it live over a WebSocket, Prometheus reads its metrics, and a broker that drops is tried again with growing waits while what the hub owed it is held.

The output on this page is its demo: a whole session played on the machine itself, the brokers replaced by stand-ins the program carries and the serial line by a pseudo-terminal, a pair of ends the system joins like a cable. Windows makes no pseudo-terminal, so there the device reaches the hub through a socket instead, and that is the one line that differs. Rebel’s mqtt, amqp and nats speak to the brokers, cbor, msgpack and protobuf decode the payloads, serial reads the line, websocket feeds the dashboard, retries spaces the attempts, and metrics counts.
