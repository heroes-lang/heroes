The Zen of Heroes

· heroes ·

Twenty lines the compiler carries inside itself. Python has import this; Heroes has heroes this.

  1. This language begins with a tribute to David Bowie.
  2. It is made for language models; people can read it too.
  3. A model may have read everything; each page must still explain itself.
  4. What the eye can see is all there is: sound and vision.
  5. There is one way to say each thing; every program sings in the same voice.
  6. That way may not be obvious at first; the Starman already knows it.
  7. The semantics are familiar; the syntax is new.
  8. Turn and face the strange: every strange choice here is deliberate.
  9. Nothing changes behind your back; every change signs its name.
  10. Every plausible mistake must become a compile error before the program runs.
  11. Unless you mark the unfinished place with ???.
  12. An honest hole beats a confident guess.
  13. A compile-time error is an answer; a runtime error is an ambush.
  14. An error should tell you how to fix the program. Anything less is a complaint.
  15. Repeating a word is cheap; forgetting one can cost the whole program.
  16. The specification stays small under pressure; every word must earn its place.
  17. Crashes and leaks are bugs. Ashes to ashes: what Heroes takes, it returns.
  18. When pretty code and safe code disagree, safe code wins.
  19. There is no standard library; C is the man who sold the world.
  20. We can be heroes, just for one day.

It ships with the compiler

This page is the language's own text, and it is a command: 

$ heroes this

Putting it in the binary keeps it with the compiler. A document drifts away from the thing it describes; a subcommand ships with it, and behind most of these lines there is a rule the compiler actually enforces. The sections below say which, and where a line states a goal rather than a rule they say that too.

Where each line comes out in practice

Lines 3 and 4 are why nothing in this language means something decided three files away: the meaning of a line comes from that line and the signature above it. The reasoning is here.

Lines 10 to 14 are the whole of the diagnostics design: a mistake caught before the program runs, a hole you can write down honestly instead of guessing, and an error that tells you the repair rather than filing a complaint. See them fire.

Line 15 is the cost formula: repeating a word is cheap, forgetting one can cost the whole program. Line 16 is the token budget: the specification is counted, not estimated, and anything added has to name what it removes. Line 17 states a goal: crashes and leaks are bugs, not accepted behaviour. Line 18 settles a conflict: when elegance and safety disagree, safety wins. Line 19 is the founding constraint, which is also why the compiler emits C.

Line 1 is the one that needs no defence, and line 20 is where the name comes from. The irony lives in the packaging: you will never meet Bowie in an error message.
