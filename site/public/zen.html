The Zen of Heroes

· heroes ·

Twenty lines the compiler carries inside itself. Python has import this; Heroes has heroes this.

  1. First of all and freely, this language is a tribute to the great David Bowie.
  2. A modern language made for LLMs, yet plain to human eyes.
  3. The machine has read everything, yet sees only the page before it.
  4. What the eye can see is all there is, nothing but sound and vision.
  5. One way to say each thing, and every program sings it in the same voice.
  6. Although that way is not obvious at first, the Starman waiting in the sky already knows it.
  7. The semantics you already know, a syntax nobody has seen before.
  8. Turn and face the strange, for every strangeness here is deliberate.
  9. Nothing changes behind your back, every change signs its name where it happens.
  10. Every plausible mistake becomes a compile error before the program ever runs.
  11. Unless you confess it honestly and write ??? in its place.
  12. An honest hole in the program beats a confident guess every time.
  13. A compile time error is an answer, a run time error is an ambush.
  14. An error tells you how to fix the program, anything less is a complaint.
  15. A repeated word is cheap, a forgotten one costs the whole program.
  16. The specification is small and lives under pressure, every word must earn its place.
  17. Nothing crashes and nothing leaks, ashes to ashes, what it takes it returns.
  18. Where the pretty things and the robust disagree, the robust wins every time.
  19. There is no standard library, everything comes from C, the man who sold the world.
  20. We can be heroes, just for one day.

It ships with the compiler

This is not a page somebody wrote about the language. It is the language's own text, and it is a command: 

$ heroes this

Which is the point of putting it in the binary rather than in a document. A document drifts away from the thing it describes; a subcommand ships with it, and every one of these lines has a rule behind it that the compiler actually enforces.

Where each line comes out in practice

Lines 3 and 4 are why nothing in this language means something decided three files away — the meaning of a line comes from that line and the signature above it. The reasoning is here.

Lines 10 to 14 are the whole of the diagnostics design: a mistake caught before the program runs, a hole you can write down honestly instead of guessing, and an error that tells you the repair rather than filing a complaint. See them fire.

Line 16 is the token budget — the specification is measured, not estimated, and an addition has to name what it removes. Line 19 is the founding constraint, which is also the reason the compiler emits C. And line 18 is a tie-break with real teeth: where elegance and robustness disagree, the language gives up the elegance.

Line 1 is the one that needs no defence, and line 20 is where the name comes from. The irony lives in the packaging: you will never meet Bowie in an error message.
