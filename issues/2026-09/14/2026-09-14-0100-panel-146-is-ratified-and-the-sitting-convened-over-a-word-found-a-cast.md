    **Origin:** panel 146, sat 2026-09-13, M-handle-verdict's last open item. The
    author reserved it at panel 139, declined both standing options at panel 145,

    **What the yes settled.** **`ptr` keeps its name**, four seats to one advisory
    objection, with the engineer's falsifier written into the row: *a compiled
    program in which a bare `ptr` reaches a position not inside and not adjacent
    to an `extern` block*. The ground is not cost — the rename is 339 lines
    across 119 files and the budget clears it at +10 vendored against 326 free —
    it is that **no seat could produce a program a rename would make correct**.
    The ffi seat compiled the whole survivor binding twice, `ptr` and
    `unsafe_ptr`, to object files that hash identically. The ergonomist wrote
    four programs from the specification alone and found the load-bearing line
    unchanged under every spelling.

    **The `opaque` family is refused BY NAME**, though the historian's survey
    converges on it and the warden priced it cheapest of all: § 13 already spends
    that word on the handle, so `db: opaque` would read as though it already were
    one, and the ffi seat measured *unnameable pointee* false at 6 of the 9
    surviving positions, where the header names the type and only Heroes cannot
    spell it.

    **And `@tail` narrowed from `ptr` to `cstr`**, which is the thing that
    mattered and is not a tidying: `@tail: ptr` makes this compiler emit
    `void ** a4` and a call that writes `(void *)a4`, a **cast**; `@tail: cstr`
    emits `const char ** a4` and hands clang the exact type to check. Robustness
    is § Precedence rank 3 and outranks every naming argument at the sitting by
    the contract's own order. **Panel 140 measured `ptr` escaping an `extern`
    group 11 times in 2 files that morning; it is now 0.**

    **Three repairs fell out and landed with it**: the emitter braced a handle
    used as a record field as though it were an aggregate, so clang warned twice
    on a correct program — a shape that existed nowhere in the tree, which is how
    the form reached step 4 without meeting it; design.md §4.19's two worked
    fences still taught the signatures defect 029 was filed against; and the
    Part 6 `raw` row's measurement.

    **Three things the sitting refused to decide**, each filed rather than
    smuggled through a sitting convened on a name: whether the **13 lines across
    7 files that recommend `ptr`** are still right after the handle form — each
    was measured at the close and each names a position no handle can reach, so
    the open question is whether a FOURTEENTH place should now recommend the
    handle where it says nothing; the **§ 3 routing clause** the ergonomist found,
    which is the defect the sitting turned out to be about, since a reader
    chooses a type in § 3 and does not meet the handle until § 13 three hundred
    lines later, both glossed with the same word; and whether **`ptr` is an FFI
    type or a core type**, which one four-line fixture with no `extern` group at
    all says it is not.
