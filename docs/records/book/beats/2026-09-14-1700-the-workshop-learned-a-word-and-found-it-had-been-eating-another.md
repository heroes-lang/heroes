2026-09-14 | M-marked-acquisition | The workshop learned a word. When you borrow something from outside — a door held open, a drawer pulled out — somebody has to put it back, and until today the workshop had no way of even knowing you had borrowed it. The word goes on the line where the lending happens, and it does one more thing than it strictly had to: it says **who takes the thing back**. That extra half was not the cheap option and it was chosen for exactly that reason, on a standing instruction to prefer the sturdier answer over the shorter one — and then it turned out to be the cheaper one anyway, by four units of the currency the panel measures in, which nobody had noticed because the person who proposed it had no way to weigh it. The other option, the clever one, was to have the workshop work it out for itself: if you told it how to put a thing back, surely it could tell when you had taken one. Five judges killed it, each with a different measurement, and the one that ended the argument was somebody opening nineteen real manuals and counting: in twelve of them, the same kind of thing is handed to you *to keep* by one instruction and *to look at* by another, and no manual anywhere says which is which. Then the same judge stopped arguing and made it happen twice — a real program, a real library, and a crash with the words *attempting free on address which was not malloc()-ed*. Under the word you write, a mistake loses you a check. Under the clever version, a mistake makes the workshop force you to throw away something that was never yours. But the part worth telling is what fell out of the building rather than out of the arguing. A rule in this place says that when a new form of writing arrives, every tool that re-reads or re-prints your work has to be taught it, one at a time, because a tool that has not learned a form does not complain — it quietly drops it. That rule fired three times in one day on this one word. Twice on the pretty-printer, which swallowed the word first in one position and then, an hour later, in the other. And once on something nobody was looking at: the thing that shows you your work as the workshop understood it had been swallowing a **different** word, a word added the previous day, and had been doing so since the hour it arrived. Nothing had noticed, and the reason is the sharpest thing here — the note written directly above that hole, by somebody who had been bitten before, says that this display is exactly what the pretty-printer checks itself against. So the guard and the guarded were blind in the same place, and a guard that cannot see agreement from correctness reports the first and means the second. The test that would have caught it did not exist, because the example it tests with had never contained either word. It does now.
