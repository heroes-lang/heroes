Things went on it when they were good ideas that would have made the language bigger, and beside each one somebody wrote the reason it could wait. Nobody had ever gone back and read the reasons. The rule for this stretch of work was that every item on the list had to be given a date and one of three answers: it comes in, and gets its own piece of work; it is refused for good, and the refusal has to name the thing that would prove it wrong; or it waits again, and the waiting has to have a condition somebody could actually check. Nine items, nine sittings. And nine times the same thing happened, which nobody had gone looking for: the item's answer was fine and the reason under it had rotted. One reason had a clock on it that had already run out. One said a thing could not be done, and eight small programs were written that afternoon doing it. One described a part of the machine in terms of another part that this workshop has never had — it was a memory of a different workshop. A note said that building a long piece of text by adding to it repeatedly is slow, and that building a list of the pieces instead is just as slow. Three ways of writing the same operation were timed side by side. One of them did a million pieces in two hundredths of a second. One of them did fifty thousand in eleven and a half seconds.
