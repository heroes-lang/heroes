2026-09-14 | M-marked-acquisition | The workshop can now be told three things about anything it borrows from outside, and it insists on being told rather than guessing. *This hands you one, and here is who takes it back.* *This takes one back.* *This lends you one, you owe nothing.* Each says one thing and none of them is worked out by the machine, which is the whole point: the clever version, where the workshop deduces from the fact that you told it how to give a thing back that it can tell when you took one, was killed by somebody opening nineteen real manuals and counting. In twelve of them the same kind of thing is handed to you to keep by one instruction and to look at by another, and the manuals do not say which is which — in one of them the two instructions sit on adjacent lines and differ by a single digit in their names. Then that person stopped arguing and made the machine do the wrong thing twice, against real libraries, and wrote down what it printed. There is a nice accident in the middle. The version that names who takes the thing back was proposed by the one person in the room with no way to weigh it, and it was assumed to cost more than the plain version because it says more. It costs less — four units less, once the plain version's two mistakes are repaired — and the mistakes were found by somebody else in the same room, one of them being that the plain version's own grammar could not describe its own example. So the sturdiest answer and the cheapest answer turned out to be one answer, which does not usually happen and is worth writing down when it does. The third word arrived last and only because it had to. A rule had been agreed: if you have told the workshop how a thing is given back, you must say, for every instruction that hands one over, which kind it is. But only two of the three words had been built, so an instruction that lends you something had no way to say so — it would have been refused for staying silent, and the only escape was to lie, after which the workshop would enforce the lie by making the program throw away something that was never its own. That is exactly the objection that had killed the clever version an hour earlier, walked back in through the answer to it. Nobody noticed while writing the decision down. It was noticed while building the thing. And the oldest lesson here fired three times in a single day: when a new way of writing arrives, every tool that re-reads your work has to be taught it separately, because a tool that has not learned something does not complain — it quietly drops it. Twice on the pretty-printer, in two different positions an hour apart.
