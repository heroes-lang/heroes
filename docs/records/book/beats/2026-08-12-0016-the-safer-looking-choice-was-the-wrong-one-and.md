2026-08-12 | M-header-constants | The safer-looking choice was the wrong one, and only building both showed it. The compiler describes each kind of declaration as one of a handful of shapes, and the new thing — a value borrowed from another language's description — could either be a new shape of its own or a widening of the shape it most resembles. A new shape looks obviously safer: this project's whole method is that a new alternative makes the compiler complain everywhere the old ones were handled, so nothing can be silently forgotten. A reviewer implemented it and found the one place that does not complain — a single line asking "is this that kind of declaration?" in a form that answers no rather than refusing to compile. A new shape there would have quietly turned every borrowed value into a reference to a function. So the widening won: it breaks fewer places loudly, and it is right in the one place that decides the answer.
