# Two sittings ratified, and both were decided against their own seats

2026-09-15. Panels 153 and 154, ratified by the author as adopted, hours after
they sat. The sittings are `docs/panel/153-*` and `docs/panel/154-*`; the
ratification entry with everything each leaves owed is in `docs/records/done/`.

## The same instrument overturned both, for the same reason

At 153 four of five seats adopted a new built-in with a spec sentence at +77
real. At 154 four of five adopted a fourth parameter word. Both sittings were
sound arguments between the options they were given, and both option sets were
short by one.

The completeness critic found the missing option each time, and each time it cost
nothing a reader must learn. At 153 it was three lines of a header of the
author's own: the shipped compiler reads the struct behind a pointer C hands back
and walks the list, output byte-identical to the equivalent C program, with no
compiler change at all. At 154 it was one string in the compile-flag list,
`-fno-delete-null-pointer-checks`, guarding the dereference where five routes
were legislating the argument.

**The tell is one sentence and the 154 critic wrote it**: *here the cheapest in
tokens and the most complete are the same route, which is the tell that the
frame, not the price, decided.* Five seats differentiated by input still share
one blind spot. At 153 the blind spot was where they looked: every seat searched
inside the compiler, and §1.11 says nothing in this language comes from there.

Owed, each with what releases it: route A's guard when a milestone next opens

**And one thing the ratification of 153 no longer owes.** Its R2 queued a second
sitting and named the one advantage a built-in would have over the header: a
guard on the null read. Panel 154 built that guard the same evening. The axis is
answered; only the spec sentence remains.
