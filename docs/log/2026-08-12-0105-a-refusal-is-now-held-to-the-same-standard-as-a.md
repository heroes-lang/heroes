2026-08-12 | **A refusal is now held to the same standard as a feature: a Part 6 row must be falsifiable by the compiler** (author decision, `/decide`; panel 039's spec-warden proposed it). The row must name the program or the compiler fact that would make it wrong, so a reader can check it rather than trust it — falsifier-as-program goes in `tests/golden/`, falsifier-as-measurement in `docs/measurements/`. The rule's home is Part 6's own preamble; CLAUDE.md §12 cites it, because it is "measurement beats opinion" applied to a refusal | the asymmetry is real and nothing else covered it: Principle 0 is a burden on what **enters** the language, so a *permanent rejection* was the one design act here subject to no burden of proof — the cheapest possible way to make the most expensive kind of commitment. The rule was proved to have teeth before it was written, twice in one afternoon: a proposed row rejecting compile-time evaluation was refuted by the emitted C of every Heroes program, each carrying two `_Static_assert(__builtin_constant_p(…))` with no `extern` in the source; and the preamble's own claim that *"each violates locality"* turned out **false of four of its own rows**, all cost-only refusals stating as much in their own reasons. Both halves landed together on the author's instruction, and that pairing is the point — a rule about falsifiability that left the document contradicting itself on the day it arrived would have been the very thing it exists to prevent | Part 6, §1.0, §1.1, CLAUDE.md §12 | 039 |
