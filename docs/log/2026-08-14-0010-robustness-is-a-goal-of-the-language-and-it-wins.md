2026-08-14 | **Robustness is a goal of the language, and it wins** (author instruction: *"uno dei motivi per cui lo voglio fare è che produca codice che non abbia problemi di segmentation fault o di memoria — tutte le scelte in quella direzione vincono su altri criteri"*). A Heroes program must not segfault and must not corrupt memory. Where a choice runs toward that, it beats every other criterion in CLAUDE.md: elegance, token cost, ergonomics, the size of the compiler, and speed. Written into §12 because it is a **precedence** rule and not a new permission — it does not suspend Principle 0, which still binds what *enters*; it decides the case where two admissible forms disagree and one of them can be made to crash. It is the mirror of §13's performance line rather than an exception to it: performance is not a goal *and* not a licence, safety is a goal *and* a tie-break. The instruction's second half is that the FFI be **complete and bug-proof** — complete because a library Heroes cannot bind is a library the author must keep C around for, bug-proof because a binding is the one place a Heroes program can reach an address nobody checked | **panel 053 was sitting when the rule arrived, and it re-ranked the sitting.** Four options were on the table about a nullable inbound `cstr`, and the ffi-pragmatist measured that three of them are blind to the only case that actually crashes: `to_str` on a null `cstr` is a **clean abort** — `runtime/parts/str.c:215` guards NULL before `strlen`, exit 134, identical at `-O0`, `-O2` and `--sanitize` — while a null `cstr` handed **straight to another C function** is `AddressSanitizer: SEGV in libsystem_platform.dylib`, and `to_str` is never called on that path. Under the old weighing that was a footnote about an unlisted case; under this rule it is the sitting's subject.
