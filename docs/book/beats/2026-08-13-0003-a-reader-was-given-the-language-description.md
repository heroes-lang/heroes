2026-08-13 | M-program-corpus | A reader was given the language description alone — no examples, no compiler, nothing else — and asked to write three small programs, then asked afterwards how it had built each string. It had avoided the slow way, and named the sentence that stopped it: the only sentence in the whole document that mentions what anything costs, and therefore, in its words, loud. That sentence had been bought a month earlier with a promise that a future reader would do exactly this, and it is the first promise of its kind this project has ever collected on. But the same reader also noticed something nobody had asked about: it had avoided the slow way and then written the *other* slow way, in two programs out of three, without noticing until it was asked. Not because the sentence was unclear — it names both — but because in two of the three tasks the language left no other route. The warning changed which words were typed and not what the program does. A caution with no alternative behind it is a caution that moves people sideways.
