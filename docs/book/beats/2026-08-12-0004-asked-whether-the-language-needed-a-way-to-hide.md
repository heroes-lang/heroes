2026-08-12 | M8a+ | Asked whether the language needed a way to hide things, the answer turned out to be that two of the three questions were not about hiding at all. Hiding a field of a record cannot work here, because building one requires naming every field, so a hidden field makes the whole thing unbuildable from outside — and because a record is a plain C structure, anybody writing the glue this language exists to write reads the hidden field at its offset, which one reviewer did, then wrote to it, then built the "hidden" case of a hidden alternative for good measure. Hiding one alternative of a choice is worse: it forces the catch-all pattern this language forbids, so whether a line compiles would depend on a declaration the reader is not allowed to see. Another language shipped exactly that, deleted it two years later as barely used, brought it back at a coarser grain five years after that, and documents the price as losing the guarantee that you handled every case. Only the third question — hiding a whole declaration from other files — was real, and it was deferred: the instrument that decides it is the port itself, which has not run.
