2026-09-11 | M-labelled-builtins | The language had a rule written into its first week: if two of a function's arguments are the same kind of thing, you have to say which is which, because nobody can see a mistake that type-checks. The compiler enforced it on everything the author wrote and, it turned out, on nothing the compiler itself provided. A blind reader found it by writing the same call two ways and being told off for neither. What made the repair worth its thousand rewritten lines was a number: the tool that imitates the classic mistake, swapping two arguments of one type, had been producing three hundred and sixty-seven programs the compiler accepted and should not have, and a hundred and fifty-nine of them were this one call. After the repair there were seventy-five, and none of them were it. The sentence that would have admitted the hole instead of closing it was priced too, and cost thirty-two times as much.
