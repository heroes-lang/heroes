2026-08-12 | M-sized-integers | A decision that looked like a matter of taste turned out to decide whether a promise was true. The author had ruled that a number too big for its box must stop the program, at every size. Implementing that meant choosing how the compiler files the eight sizes internally — eight separate entries, or one entry with a size written on it — and the two options differ in nothing a user could ever see. A reviewer measured it instead of arguing: with eight entries, the line that decides whether to install the overflow check simply does not recognise the smaller sizes, so it silently omits the check and the program runs to completion with a wrong answer. With one entry, the same line refuses to compile until somebody handles it. The promise the author made was true only under the second filing.
