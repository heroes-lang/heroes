## 1. The sources, and what each states

### 1.1 Csmith: the checksum, the undefined behaviours and where the bugs were

The paper states *more than 325 previously unknown bugs* reported across 11
compilers, of which *79 GCC bugs and 202 LLVM bugs*, the LLVM figure *about
2% of all LLVM bug reports*, twenty-five GCC bugs marked P1. Its definitions
are the ones this milestone's oracles use: a *compile-time crash error*
occurs *whenever the compiler process exits with a status other than zero or
fails to produce executable output*; a *wrong-code error* is a wrong result,
a crash of the generated code, the termination of a program that should run
for ever or the non-termination of one that should end; a **silent
wrong-code error** is one *produced without any sort of warning from the
compiler*. The oracle is a checksum: the generated `main` *computes a
checksum of the non-pointer global variables, prints the checksum, and
exits*, and the compilers under test must agree (differential testing). The
programs *must not execute any of the 191 kinds of undefined behavior* nor
*depend on any of the 52 kinds of unspecified behavior* of C99; Table 1 lists
the strategies, use without initialization (explicit initializers), infinite
recursion (disallowed), signed overflow (bounded loop variables and safe
math wrappers), out-of-bounds access (indices forced in bounds), unspecified
evaluation order and read/write conflicts between sequence points (effect
analysis), out-of-scope and null pointers (pointer analysis and checks).
*About 10% of the programs generated by Csmith are (apparently)
non-terminating*, handled by timeouts.

Where the bugs were, Table 4: front end 0 (GCC) and 10 (LLVM), middle end 49
and 75, back end 17 and 74, unclassified 13 and 43. Tables 5 and 6 split the
located ones into wrong-code and crash: GCC 29 and 50 over 25 files, LLVM 66
and 136 over 82 files, constant folding, instruction combining, dead-code and
dead-store elimination, range propagation and register coalescing at the
top. Table 2 counts the bugs that manifest *when compiler optimizations are
disabled*: GCC 4 and LLVM 19, *pernicious because it defeats the conventional
wisdom that compiler bugs can be avoided by turning off the optimizer*. And
the one finding about size: bug-finding power *found at a surprisingly large
program size: about 81 KB*, for optimizers.

### 1.2 YARPGen: values tracked while generating, and generation policies

*More than 220 bugs in GCC, LLVM, and the Intel C++ Compiler*: *83 new bugs
in GCC, 62 in LLVM, and 76 in the Intel C++ Compiler*. Two kinds of bug are
its concern, the crash and the *wrong code bug, or a miscompilation*, since
*compilers can go wrong in other ways, such as looping forever, but in our
experience, these failures are less common*. Its first contribution is the move
this milestone's page names: each expression is type-checked bottom-up
and then *the values being processed by the expression are analyzed*: *Values
are tracked concretely: YARPGen does not use a conservative abstract
interpreter. This is possible because the generated code is loop-free*, and
*when YARPGen detects an operation with undefined behavior, it performs a local
fix, rewriting the operation into a form that does not trigger UB* (Table 1:
a shift by too much becomes a shift by less, an overflow a safe neighbour),
the rewrites checked by *exhaustive testing of 4-bit values* and a subset
verified in Z3. The oracle is again a checksum over the outputs, compared
across compilers and optimization levels.

Its second contribution is **generation policies**, *mechanisms for
systematically altering the character of the emitted code*, drawn *from our
knowledge of how optimizing compilers work*: six operator contexts, additive,
bitwise, logical, multiplicative, bitwise-shift and additive-multiplicative,
applied to random regions of an expression tree; a constants policy, all or
half the leaves constants, constants of small magnitude and near `INT_MAX`
more often than uniform ones, constants with contiguous blocks of zeroes and
ones, a buffer of earlier constants reused with their negations and
complements; a common-subexpression buffer, reusing earlier subexpressions;
and parameter shuffling, *23 different parameters* whose distributions are
themselves drawn at random per test (types, leaves, where results go, array
representation, operators, statement kinds, how many subexpressions). The
bug it names as the policy's witness is LLVM's bit-tracking dead code
elimination (LLVM 33695), *very difficult to trigger without combining the
shift-bitwise context with half of expression leaves being constants*. Its
Table 2 heads read as this catalogue needs: each bug a *Type* (`wrong code`
or `ICE`) and a *Component* (tree-optimization, rtl-optimization,
middle-end, target, sanitizer, c, c++), several *at -O0*, several the
sanitizer's own false positives.

**The live list, counted today** (`bugs.rst`, the linked rows summed per
section): LLVM 93, GCC 152, ISPC 12, MSVC 8, OpenWatcom 3, Alive2 2, TCC 1,
271 in all, the list stating no total and no date.

### 1.3 EMI, and whether fuzzer-found bugs matter

Equivalence modulo inputs (Le, Afshari, Su, PLDI 2014): a deterministic
program and its profile under some inputs; *stochastically prune its
unexecuted code*; the pruned program must behave the same on those inputs,
so the oracle needs no answer. The project's page states *GCC/LLVM bugs:
1,602 (total) / 1,007 (fixed)*, no date; the PLDI 2014 figure read second
hand is 147 confirmed bugs in eleven months, most miscompilations.

Marcozzi et al. measure the impact: *more than 10 million lines of C/C++
code from 309 Debian packages*, compiled with *12% of the historical and now
fixed miscompilation bugs found by four state-of-the-art fuzzers in the
Clang/LLVM compiler, as well as 18 bugs found by human users*; their Table 1
counts the Clang/LLVM bugs each tool had reported, Csmith 164 (52 fixed
miscompilations), EMI 783 (163), Orange 12 (7), yarpgen 66 (4). *Almost half
of the fuzzer-found bugs propagate to the generated binaries for at least one
package, in which case only a very small part of the binary is typically
affected, yet causing two failures when running the test suites*;
*user-reported and formal verification bugs do not exhibit a higher impact*.
So a bug a generator finds is worth what a bug a user finds is worth, by the
one study that asked.

### 1.4 The survey, and the bug studies it carries

The survey orders the field by how programs are made, generation-based,
transformation-based, two-pronged, learning-based, and by the oracle,
differential testing (several compilers, or several configurations of one)
and metamorphic testing (EMI among them). Its § 3 carries the studies this
catalogue could not read directly. Sun et al., *about 50K bugs and 30K bug
fixes* of GCC and LLVM: *the bug-revealing test cases are typically small.
80% of them have fewer than 45 lines of code*; *most bug fixes only modify a
single source file*; lifetimes of 200 days (GCC) and 111 (LLVM); *C++
component is the most buggy*. Zhou et al., *8771 and 1564 optimization bugs
of GCC and LLVM*: optimizations the second most bug-prone component after
C++; *the value range propagation optimization and the instruction
combination optimization are the two most buggy*; *57.21% of the GCC
optimization bugs and 61.38% of the LLVM optimization bugs are
mis-optimization bugs*. The Java-family front-end study, 320 typing-related
bugs in Java, Scala, Kotlin and Groovy: five symptoms, *Unexpected
Compile-time Error, Internal Compiler Error, Unexpected Runtime Behavior,
Misleading Report, and Compilation Performance Issue*; five root causes, type
related, semantic analysis, resolution, error handling and reporting, AST
transformation; *parameterized types, type argument inference, and
parameterized class are the top three language features* that trigger them;
*89% of all studied bugs could be fixed within 100 lines of code* and still
took *186 days on average*. The deep-learning compilers (603 bugs): *crash is
the most common symptom, accounting for 59.37%*, *wrong code the second
(25.04%)*.

### 1.5 Young compilers: rustc, Zig, Go, Nim

**rustc** (arXiv 2503.23985, the abstract): issues of 2022 to 2024, *a manual
review of 301 valid issues*; the bugs *primarily arise from Rust's type
system and lifetime model*, frequent in the HIR and MIR modules; *both valid
and invalid programs can trigger bugs*; *existing testing tools struggle to
detect non-crash errors*. **Zig** (issue 10121, open since 2021-11-09, four
comments): *13 unique* compiler crashes found by an AFL-based compiler fuzzer
(`agroce/afl-compiler-fuzzer`), minimized where possible, *compiler probably
shouldn't crash for these inputs*. **Go** (`test/fixedbugs` at `master`):
2,144 entries, 1,896 `.go` files, 1,584 named `issueNNNN`, 533 `bugNNN`, 18
`gccNNN`, one regression program per fixed bug, which is the shape
`tests/golden/*/fixedbugs-*` already has. **Nim** (`tests/` at `devel`): 122
folders, among them `ccgbugs` 135 files and `ccgbugs2` 9, the bugs of its C
code generator, the nearest cousin of `selfhost/emit/`; `parser` 48, `lexer`
20, `overflow` 7, `errmsgs` 177, `generics` 149.

## 4. What the catalogue says to the generator, and what the sitting should judge

- **Generate from the type and compute the value as the program is built.**
  Both sources do it (Csmith's checksum over the globals, YARPGen's value
  tracking), and both say why: without the value the silent class B is
  invisible, and with it no second compiler is needed as judge. For Heroes
  the value is also what decides the arm: an operation whose operands would
  abort goes to the declared arm, with the abort's message as its answer,
  which is how the guard rails of shape I get checked rather than assumed.
- **Two arms, never a rewrite.** YARPGen rewrites an unsafe operation into a
  safe neighbour because C's undefined behaviour must never run; Heroes has
  none, so the unsafe operation is kept in a program of its own whose
  expected output is the abort, and the clean arm picks operands that make it
  safe. The list of what aborts is the specification's (§ 6, § 7, § 10,
  § 13), not a table of this document.
- **Policies are what keep a generator from saturating**, and YARPGen's are
  reusable almost as written: operator contexts over regions of an expression
  tree, constants near the edges of each width and with runs of bits, a
  buffer of reused subexpressions, and parameters shuffled per program (its
  23). Clang's optimizer at `-O2` is the one under test through the emitted
  C, so the contexts aimed at instcombine and reassociation are aimed at the
  right place.
- **Many small programs, not few large ones**, except on the two dials. Sun's
  finding (80% of bug-revealing tests under 45 lines) and the reducer's cost
  both say so; Csmith's 81 KB is an optimizer's sweet spot. Depth (K) and the
  C boundary (L) are the two dials the generator turns up on purpose, and
  the clang limits of § 2.3 say where the dial meets the C compiler rather
  than ours, which oracle 1 must tell apart (a clang death on the program's
  C is a defect of the emitter's shape, filed as such, not a generator
  artefact).
- **The C boundary needs a header generator beside the program generator**:
  an `extern` group is valid only against a header, so the generator writes
  both, in the shapes § 13 of the specification admits (a struct by
  value, a `tag`, a `partial`, a union, an out-parameter, a `counted_by`
  array, a callback, a `cstr` in and out, `owned`, the handle marks), and the
  C half is compiled with the program. This is where 26 of 38 falls and 19 of
  30 silent defects were, so it is the first arm to build after the core.
- **What a generator of valid programs cannot see, said once**: shape D
  (accepting a wrong program) and shape T (a diagnostic that misleads) are
  the mutation instrument's, `heroes mutate` and the recovery plan; this
  milestone does not re-implement them and the sitting should not ask it to.
- **Two oracles that need no answer, beside the five**: the agreement of the
  three configurations (shape C, Goose's pass on and off, the only
  differential arm while there is one backend), already among the five as
  oracle 2's configurations; and EMI's pruning of unexecuted arms (a `match`
  arm the computed value never takes may be replaced by `_ = 0` and the
  program must print the same), not planned by the row and left here as the
  cheapest addition if the five run dry.
- **The sixth oracle stays dated**: two backends, when M-qbe-backend exists.

## 5. The two numbers, as the row's page asked for them

The milestone page of 2026-09-06 asked that *the two shapes the author named,
the C boundary and depth, enter it with a number beside them rather than as
an impression*. Over the 581 defects of the compiler's areas on 2026-10-28:
the C boundary 151 (26 per cent), holding 26 of the 38 falls and 19 of the 30
silent defects; depth 31 (5.3 per cent), holding 3 of the 38 falls, none of
the silent defects, and 16 `improvement` items, the cost class. The author
named the right two, and the catalogue orders them: the C boundary first,
because it is where the compiler is wrong; depth second, because it is where
the compiler and clang are slow, and where one of them dies.
