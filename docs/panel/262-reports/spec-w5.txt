Where C leaves a callback's frames without their returning (by a jump, an exception or a coroutine it never resumes, say), a value they held of more than 4,096 bytes, or holding a `partial` record, and a buffer they lent to C stay held until the program ends, counted with the live ones against 1 GiB for the whole program, past which it aborts, saying how many callbacks have not returned; and where it leaves a signal handler of its own any way but by its return or a jump of the C library's back to a call the signal interrupted that this runtime can tell left it (by assembly, a `swapcontext`, or a jump into a coroutine the signal did not interrupt or into the handler's own frames, say), a callback called later on that handler's thread may abort, saying the handler was not seen to end, or that C called it from inside one, as one called from inside the handler may.
