A handler of C's is over where it returns, or where `longjmp`, `siglongjmp`, `_longjmp` or a `setcontext` to a context `getcontext` took carries the thread out of it onto the thread's own stack, and a callback then runs; after a handler left any other way (by a jump no function of the C library makes, written in assembly say, by a `swapcontext` away from it, by a jump onto a stack `makecontext` made, or by a jump that lands within it) a callback called later on its thread may abort, saying the handler was not seen to end, or that C called it from inside one.
